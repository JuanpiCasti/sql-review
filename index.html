Postgres, from the ground up

Nineteen short chapters covering the parts of Postgres worth knowing well — the type system and schema, the relational algebra Postgres actually runs, the concurrency control underneath it, the indexing and planning that determine whether a query takes a millisecond or a minute, and the operational machinery that keeps it running.

This is a study reference, not a tutorial. Each chapter assumes you have written SQL before and want the Postgres-specific mental model — the lock modes, the snapshot semantics, the frame clauses, the index types — laid out cleanly enough to review. Examples are short. Result tables are real. Pitfalls are the ones that have actually cost engineers a weekend.

Read it cover to cover, or jump to whichever chapter you most need to refresh. Every chapter ends with a one-card cheat sheet for the next time you forget what NOT IN does with a NULL.

A note on versions Everything here targets PostgreSQL 16+. Where behaviour changed in a recent version (notably CTE materialization in 12) it is called out in the relevant chapter.
